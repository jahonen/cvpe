Between 50,000 and 60,000 people crossed from Morocco into Ceuta on July 31st 2026, directed by Moroccan police. At least 72 confirmed dead. Almost all returned within 48 hours. Spain managed the crossing without EU assistance and emerged politically stronger. See the full analysis: [Ceuta: The First Evergray Battle](/essays/ceuta-first-evergray-battle/).

The EU institutional response:

Von der Leyen issued a statement calling the images "unacceptable" and tasked two Commissioners to support Spain. No expression of solidarity with Spain as the frontline member state under hybrid attack. No formal characterisation of Morocco's documented role in directing the crossing. No rebuke of member states threatening illegal Schengen suspension.

Italy suspended Schengen with Spain — a move legal analysts described as incompatible with current treaty architecture. Twenty-two member state leaders signed a letter condemning Spain's migration management. An emergency ministerial meeting produced no decisions.

Spain had already resolved the crisis before the ministerial meeting convened — [proximity politics](/lexicon/proximity-politics/) in operation.

**What followed shows this was not a one-off delay but a genuine institutional failure to resolve an intra-EU rupture.** Italy's suspension, framed as a temporary security measure, hardened into a mutual month-long standoff: Spain reinstated its own border checks on Italian travellers from 9 August, running to at least 7 September. The bloc of 22 states did not soften — Finland and Denmark backed suspension outright, Sweden and Austria reserved the right to act unilaterally. Italy's own interior minister told the Schengen parliamentary committee on 13 August that restored controls had produced three arrests and 21 rejected third-country nationals in two weeks — a yield that supports the reading, offered at the time by CEPS researcher Davide Colombi, that this was political theatre rather than functioning security policy. The predicted second crossing wave arrived on schedule on 15 August; Moroccan police intercepted it 3km from the border, and roughly 350 people were arrested, several reportedly destined for desert deportation zones on the Algerian border — extending, not resolving, the pattern this audit already documents. As of 17 August, Italy has not lifted the suspension despite its own stated review floor of "not before 15 August."

At no point did the Commission or any EU-level mechanism actually resolve the Italy-Spain standoff. Von der Leyen's letter, sent 3 August once Spain's unilateral management had already made the framing safe, remains the only institutional intervention on record three weeks later.

**The charge:** EU solidarity with a member state under hybrid attack, and EU capacity to resolve the resulting intra-member-state rupture, were both absent throughout — replaced by a single statement issued once the underlying crisis no longer required anything of the institution making it.
