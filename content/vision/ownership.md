If you want to call something European, you need to [actually own and control it](/lexicon/ownership-control-criterion/). Not just register a company here. Not just fly a flag over it. Real ownership, real control, or it doesn't count as sovereign.

Too much of what gets called "European" today is [actually owned or controlled somewhere else](/lexicon/sovereignty-washing/). That's not sovereignty. It's [the appearance of sovereignty](/audit/digital-sovereignty-package/), borrowed for as long as it's convenient for whoever actually holds the reins.

This applies to companies, to infrastructure, to technology, to data. The test is always the same: who actually decides what happens to this thing? If the honest answer isn't "us," then it isn't ours, no matter what the paperwork says.
