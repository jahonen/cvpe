Some things genuinely need to be unified at the European level, with one real chain of command and one shared budget, not fragmented into twenty-seven competing versions. Defense is the clearest example: Europe needs a real, unified force, with [its own funding that isn't hostage to any single country's politics](/audit/nato-gdp-metric/).

But unifying something doesn't mean erasing what makes each part distinct. A tapestry is woven from many different threads, each keeping its own color, and the whole thing only looks like one picture from a distance. Up close, every thread is still exactly itself.

So a unified European defense force should keep national traditions, insignia, and identities alive as real, visible threads, not blend them into one uniform color. [It should let regions specialize in what they're naturally good at](/essays/highway-is-the-doctrine/), arctic and artillery expertise here, mountain warfare there, rather than every country trying to duplicate everything. And authority within it should follow [the same logic as proximity politics](/a-certain-vision/proximity-politics/): a clear, shared strategic intent set at the top, with real tactical authority held by the commanders actually closest to the situation on the ground.

This same weaving approach isn't just for defense. It's the method for resolving the pull between the other stars: [public ownership](/a-certain-vision/natural-monopolies/) pulls toward centralizing, [proximity politics](/a-certain-vision/proximity-politics/) pulls toward local judgment, and the tapestry is how both stay true at once, one pattern, held together at the center, woven from threads that never stop being themselves.
