**Ahonen's Razor** *(n.)* — Political analysis requires separating the problem, the attribution, and the solution. Conflating them produces bad policy.

Three questions, kept strictly apart:

1. **Problem** — What is actually happening? (Measurable, falsifiable, ideally boring.)
2. **Attribution** — Who or what is causing it? (An evidence question, not a loyalty question.)
3. **Solution** — What would change the outcome? (An engineering question, judged by results.)

Most political dysfunction begins by fusing these. The commonest fusion runs backwards: choose the villain first (attribution), infer the problem from the villain's existence, and select the solution that most punishes the villain rather than the one that most changes the outcome. The result is policy that is emotionally satisfying and operationally useless — and, in the [evergray](/lexicon/evergray-wars) environment, actively dangerous, because adversaries deliberately construct incidents whose attribution is ambiguous precisely to exploit polities that cannot discuss a problem without first agreeing on a villain.

The razor also cuts the opposite failure: solutions adopted without an attribution at all — the "lessons learned" report that names no one and therefore changes nothing.

## Development

Developed across 2026 in essays and threads, first as an informal discipline ("problem, attribution, solution — pick one per sentence") applied to migration debates, where the fusion is near-total: the problem (irregular arrivals at manufactured pressure points), the attribution (state instrumentalisation vs. structural push factors — an empirical question with an actual answer), and the solution (border doctrine, see [Proximity Politics](/lexicon/proximity-politics)) are routinely collapsed into a single tribal signal.

## Implications

Applied consistently, the razor makes positions portable across tribes: one can hold that migration pressure is partly manufactured (attribution) without holding any particular view of migrants (problem framing), and support hard border capability (solution) while opposing heritage nationalism. Every essay on this site is written under the razor; the [Accusation Audit](/audit) is its institutional form — attribution with evidence, dated, and revised when the evidence moves.
