A Certain Vision

Europe has been through a lot. Empire, war, collapse, and rebuilding. Most of what gets written about Europe today is about what's wrong, or what we should be against. That matters, but it isn't enough on its own.

This section says clearly what we actually want Europe to become next.

It's built around seven ideas. We call them the seven stars. Each one stands on its own, but they only really make sense as a set, the way stars only make a shape when you see them together.

One thing holds several of these ideas together: a method we call the tapestry. Some things need to be unified at the European level. Other things work best decided close to home, by the people who actually live with the consequences. Those two instincts can pull in opposite directions. The tapestry is how you get both at once: one pattern, woven from many threads, each thread keeping its own color. From a distance it looks like one Europe. Up close, every nation and every region is still exactly itself.

Below are the seven stars. There's also a page telling the story behind all of this, and an open invitation to artists who want to help tell it better than we can.

The Seven Stars

Star One · Betelgeuse

Ownership and Control

If you want to call something European, you need to actually own and control it. Real ownership, real control, or it doesn't count as sovereign.

Star Two · Bellatrix

Institutional Trust, Earned Not Inherited

We trust our institutions because they answer to us, not because they've always existed. Trust is earned by accountability, performance, and honesty in failure.

Star Three · Rigel

Civic Belonging as Practice

Being part of Europe is something you do, not just something you are. Belonging is built through participation, not conferred at birth.

Star Four · Saiph

Proximity Politics

Decisions belong closest to whoever actually feels their impact. Moving a decision upward needs a real reason, not just habit.

Star Five · Alnitak

Public Ownership of Natural Monopolies

Some things work better in public hands because no real competition is possible for them. A market can't discipline a monopoly. It can only extract from one.

Star Six · Alnilam

A Fair Deal Between Public and Private

When public money underwrites a genuinely big private risk, the public deserves a permanent stake in the returns — not a grant that quietly becomes someone's private fortune.

Star Seven · Mintaka

One Pattern, Many Threads

Some things must be unified at the European level. Unifying them doesn't mean erasing what makes each part distinct. One pattern, woven from threads that keep their own colour.
