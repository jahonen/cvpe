A Certain Vision

The Story Behind the Seven Stars

Once, there walked a giant who believed there was nothing on earth he could not claim. He hunted without limit, because he had never been taught that limit was a kind of wisdom rather than a wall. This was Europe's oldest boast: empire mistaken for destiny, dominion mistaken for order. And as it always does, the boast summoned its own correction. Two wars fought on its own soil. The slow collapse of everything the giant thought was permanent. The scorpion doesn't punish out of cruelty. It simply proves that no appetite is without limit, no matter how magnificent the hunter.

But the story doesn't end with the fall. What came after wasn't a return to what existed before. It was something closer to resurrection, a body that had genuinely been broken, choosing to come back changed. And here is the part that asks for no faith at all, only honesty: the wounds weren't hidden. They were kept visible, on purpose, and they became the very frame that holds the new shape together. That's the harder miracle. Not that the giant died, but that what rose from it built its strength from an admitted failure rather than a denied one.

Seven points of light mark that new shape. Not commandments handed down from on high, but a constellation, meaning something only when read together. One marks that sovereignty belongs to whoever truly owns and controls a thing. One marks that institutions earn trust through accountability, not birthright. One marks that belonging is a practice, lived, not a status conferred at birth. One marks that legitimacy lives closest to whoever feels the pressure, not furthest away in some capital. One marks that what nature made a monopoly cannot be left to a market that can only extract from it, never improve it. One marks a new partnership between public patience and private daring, so no bold venture is so fragile that its failure ruins the many, and no success is so complete that it escapes the many who paid for the risk. And one marks a pattern woven from many threads, each thread keeping its own color, unmistakably one only when seen from a distance.

What comes next isn't a decree, because a decree only ever binds the generation that receives it. It's closer to a covenant: a promise that has to be renewed, in every generation, by the people who actually choose to live beneath that light. That's the only kind of permanence an honest people can offer each other. Not a monument. A promise, kept, and kept again.

An Invitation to European Artists

This story, and the seven ideas it carries, is a policy vision first. It was written to be argued with, tested, and improved, not admired from a distance. But every vision worth keeping also needs to be felt, not just read, and that isn't something a policy paper can do on its own, and it isn't something its author is equipped to do either.

So this is an open invitation. If you're an artist, anywhere in Europe, and you read these seven stars and find yourself in agreement with what they ask for, the reasoning behind them and the trade-offs they accept, we would welcome your help turning this into something people can feel. A painting. A piece of music. A poem. A film. Anything. This page will grow into a gallery of what comes back.
