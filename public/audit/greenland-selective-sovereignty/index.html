EU Response to US Greenland Territorial Claims — Selective Sovereignty

Standard: Consistent application of sovereignty, territorial integrity, and inviolability of borders — the same principles invoked for Ukraine

VERDICT: PARTIAL · Published August 2026 · Updated August 2026

From late 2025 through January 2026, the United States President made explicit and repeated territorial claims over Greenland — an autonomous territory of Denmark, a NATO founding member, and an EU-adjacent Arctic space. Trump stated the US would obtain Greenland "one way or the other" and until January 21st refused to rule out military force. A Republican bill was introduced to authorise Trump to "take such steps as may be necessary" to annex Greenland.

The European response was substantive in language and inadequate in architecture.

Five larger EU states plus Denmark and the UK issued a joint statement on January 6th stressing "sovereignty, territorial integrity and the inviolability of borders." An extraordinary informal European Council meeting was convened January 22nd. António Costa stated the EU would "not accept violations of international law" and that Denmark and Greenland had the EU's full support. Macron said he "cannot imagine a scenario in which the United States would be placed in a position to violate Danish sovereignty."

The language was correct. The architecture was absent.

No EU institution invoked the specific legal mechanisms available — trade instrument deployment, technology access restrictions, formal Article 42.7 TEU consultations. Chatham House noted Europe had "important leverage it should be prepared to use" — and did not use. The ICDS assessment was precise: "NATO and the EU [were] de facto and de jure compromised." Belgian PM De Wever was the most direct European voice: "Europe cannot be a miserable slave to Trump" — and that the US should be regarded as an ally only if it acts as one.

The structural failure is the asymmetry of application. The EU invoked the identical principles — sovereignty, territorial integrity, inviolability of borders — regarding Russia and Ukraine with full institutional machinery: sanctions, asset freezes, arms supply, ICC referral, trade restrictions. When the territorial aggressor was Washington rather than Moscow, the same principles produced joint statements and an informal Council meeting.

The principles are not the problem. The selective application is the audit finding.

The charge: Europe applies the rules-based international order symmetrically in rhetoric and asymmetrically in practice — based on the identity of the violator rather than the nature of the violation.

Evidence

  • Trump: would obtain Greenland "one way or the other," refused to rule out military force until January 21st
  • Joint statement January 6th — France, UK, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain, Denmark
  • Extraordinary European Council January 22nd — Costa statement
  • Chatham House: Europe has leverage it should use
  • ICDS/CEPS: NATO and EU "de facto and de jure compromised"
  • De Wever: "miserable slave to Trump"
  • Republican Greenland annexation bill, January 2026
  • Contrast: EU response to Russian territorial aggression in Ukraine — full sanctions architecture, arms supply, ICC referral
