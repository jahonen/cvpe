Von der Leyen / Morocco Non-Response

Standard: EU external relations integrity; accurate characterisation of threats to member state sovereignty

VERDICT: PARTIAL · Published 4 August 2026 · Updated 4 August 2026

New York Times reporters on the ground in Ceuta on July 31st 2026 confirmed, through named on-the-record testimony, that Moroccan police officers directed civilians toward the border crossing point. Officers told civilians "Go that way, go that way." Another told a man approaching the border "Come to Spain."

Video footage circulating on social media, identified by observers familiar with Moroccan police uniforms from direct experience, showed uniformed personnel assisting people from trucks on roads approaching the Ceuta border.

Von der Leyen's statement of July 31st characterised the event as an irregular migration challenge requiring rule-based response. It did not name Morocco. It did not characterise Morocco's documented operational role. It did not invoke EU external relations instruments in response to a member state being subjected to a state-directed hybrid operation.

Two weeks before the crossing, the US House of Representatives passed legislation embedding language formally describing Ceuta and Melilla as "located in Moroccan territory," allocating $40 million in military financing to Morocco, and calling for US mediation of their "future status." The State Department subsequently blamed Spain for the crossing. Von der Leyen did not address the American dimension publicly.

The verdict is partial because the Commission's eventual solidarity letter (August 3rd) represented a partial correction — but the failure to name the actors in the initial response was the consequential moment.

The charge: The Commission President declined to name a state-directed hybrid operation against EU territory as what it was.

Evidence

  • NYT testimony: Youssef Alaoui, 26, and Mohammed Ahmidho, 37, named sources, July 31 2026
  • Von der Leyen statement July 31, 2026
  • US House Appropriations Committee report language, July 2026
  • US State Department statement July 31, 2026
